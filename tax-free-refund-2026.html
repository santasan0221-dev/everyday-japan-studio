How Japan's Tax-Free Refund System Works (Since November 2026)

Checked against official Japanese sources · Updated November 2026

If you last shopped tax-free in Japan before November 2026, unlearn the old routine. Showing your passport at the register and paying a tax-free price no longer exists. For purchases made on or after 1 November 2026, Japan runs a refund system — and the date of your purchase, not your flight, decides which system applies.

The new flow, in three steps

  1. You pay the full, tax-included price at the store. Nothing is deducted at the register. This is correct under the new system, not a mistake by the shop.
  2. On departure day, Customs confirms that you are taking the goods out of Japan.
  3. After that confirmation, the store (or the refund operator it uses) refunds the consumption tax in the price.

Two consequences follow. First, the refund is not automatic: skip the Customs step, or do it in the wrong order, and there is no refund — and it cannot be fixed after you fly. Second, Customs never pays you. Customs only confirms; questions about money go to the store where you bought the goods.

What changed — and what was abolished

The old split between "general goods" and "consumables" is gone, and with it the ¥500,000 cap on consumables, the special sealed packaging, and the rule that goods had to be for daily use. Everything you buy now counts together. What is not eligible at all: gold and platinum bullion, gold and platinum coins, and goods that are exempt from consumption tax in the first place.

The threshold is ¥5,000 or more before tax, at the same store, on the same day. Food, cosmetics and clothing combine freely, and several purchases at the same store on the same day can be added together. In tax-included terms, the threshold is roughly ¥5,500 for 10% goods or ¥5,400 for 8% food items. Most visitors qualify under a Temporary Visitor stamp; residents of Japan, people working in Japan, and anyone who has been in Japan for six months or more cannot use the system.

How much do you actually get back?

The refund is the consumption tax inside the price you paid — not 10% of the sticker price. Most goods carry 10% tax; takeaway food and non-alcoholic drinks carry the reduced 8% rate. The formulas:

  • 10% goods: price × 10/110 — so ¥11,000 of souvenirs contains ¥1,000 of tax.
  • 8% goods: price × 8/108 — so ¥5,400 of snacks contains ¥400 of tax.

One honest warning: the government does not set, cap or publish refund handling fees, and the official rules set no deadline for payment. Some operators deduct a service fee, and at least one airport terminal operator states that amounts under ¥100 are not refunded. So ask two questions at every register: "Is there a handling fee, and how much?" and "When and how will I receive the refund?" Your refund method — bank transfer, card, app, or cash at the airport — is registered at the store when you buy, and it depends on the store.

Departure day: the order that matters

Customs confirmation happens before you check in or drop your bags. Once a bag is checked in, it cannot be brought back for inspection — this is the single most expensive mistake in the new system.

  1. Go to a tax-free procedure terminal in the international departure lobby of the airport you leave Japan from, and scan your passport. At seven airports (Narita, Haneda, Kansai, Chubu Centrair, New Chitose, Fukuoka, Naha) you can instead use Visit Japan Web on your phone over the departure-lobby Wi-Fi, before security — if you hold Temporary Visitor, Diplomat or Official status and registered in advance.
  2. Green means you are done. Red means take all your tax-free goods to the Customs Inspection Area and present them to an officer. Walking away from a red result means you were never confirmed — and there is no refund.
  3. Only now check in and drop your bags. After confirmation, the goods may go in checked baggage or stay with you.
  4. If your store's refund method is airport cash, the refund point is typically after passport control. Card, app and bank refunds are processed by the store afterwards.

The traps that cancel refunds

  • Confirmation works receipt by receipt. If even one item from a receipt is not with you, nothing on that receipt is refunded.
  • Do not consume consumables. Eating one snack or using one face cream cancels the entire receipt it was bought on. If you did consume something, skip the terminal and tell a Customs officer at the counter instead.
  • Keep the packaging on general goods where practical — Customs must be able to identify the item as the one you bought.
  • Never ship purchases home separately. Goods sent by post or courier get no refund; you must carry them out yourself.
  • Mind the 90-day deadline. Confirmation must happen within 90 days of purchase, counting the day after purchase as day 1 — a purchase on 1 November has a deadline of 30 January.
  • Check your passport stamp. If you entered through an automated immigration gate, make sure you received the landing-permission stamp; without proof of your status and entry date, a store cannot verify that you qualify.

Airport details vary — kiosk locations and hours differ by airport and terminal, and at least one major airport's own website still described the old system as of November 2026. Before you travel, check the Japan Customs map of tax refund kiosks and inspection areas for your airport.
